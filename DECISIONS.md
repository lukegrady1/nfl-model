## Phase 0

- **Extra columns kept** beyond the spec list: `drive`, `fixed_drive` (spec asks for them), `game_date`, `home_score`, `away_score`, `wp`, `vegas_wp` (Phase 2 filters on pre-snap win probability), `desc`, `td_team`, `defensive_two_point_conv`, `return_team`, kick/punt detail flags. Reason: validation and the Phase 1 tables need them; nothing was removed from the spec list.
- **Marker rows** (`play_type` null: GAME, END QUARTER, END GAME, Two-Minute Warning) are used to compute `seconds_elapsed` and `next_is_two_minute_warning`, then dropped from `plays.parquet`.
- **`seconds_elapsed`** = this row's `game_seconds_remaining` minus the next row's (any row type, including timeout rows, so a play followed by a timeout records only the play's own duration). Null across halftime, the end of regulation and OT period boundaries. Clipped to [0, 60].
- **Clock-stopper approximation:** out of bounds counts as a stop only when the clock at the snap is under 2:00 of the first half or 5:00 of the second half (NFL rule: otherwise the clock restarts on the ready-for-play signal). Also treated as stops: incomplete passes, spikes, scores, changes of possession (interception, lost fumble, punt, kickoff, failed 4th down), accepted penalties, and plays followed by the two-minute warning.
- **nflfastR codes the receiving team as `posteam` on kickoff rows.** Start-spot tables use `next_scrim_posteam` / `next_scrim_yardline` (the next scrimmage snap), so kickoffs and punts are handled identically.
- **Two-point tries** (`two_point_attempt == 1`, play_type pass/run) are classified `two_point` before the pass/run rules so they never enter the play-call or outcome tables.
- **Score check mismatches** (3 games): all are 2-point swaps where a safety was scored by the original offense after a same-play turnover. The validation attributes safeties to `defteam`; the real play has two possession changes. Left as logged mismatches (0.13% < 1%).
- **Summary definitions:** offensive plays per team-game = pass + run + kneel + spike; pass call rate = pass / (pass + run) excluding kneels and spikes; sack and scramble rates per dropback; interception and completion rates per attempt (complete + incomplete + interception). Extra target columns were added (`margin_std`, `ot_share`, `tie_share`, `home_margin_mean`, `fg_make_rate`, `drives_per_team_game`, `two_point_share`) because the Phase 1 acceptance needs OT share and margin std.
- **2026 is in progress** (79 regular-season games through week 6 in the cache). It is always refreshed on ingest and is included in `plays.parquet`, but the Phase 1 tables use 2021-2025 only (`table_seasons`).

## Phase 1

- **Overtime rules check:** the official rulebook pages on operations.nfl.com returned 404 to the fetcher, so the config values were verified against several secondary sources that quote Rule 16 (ESPN, NBC/PFT, Fox Sports explainers for 2025-26). Confirmed: regular season = one 10-minute period, both teams get a possession unless the first possession ends with a defensive touchdown or a safety, sudden death after both have possessed, ties allowed, 2 timeouts per team; playoffs = 15-minute periods until there is a winner, 3 timeouts. Settings kept as written. Simplifications: playoff OT timeouts reset every period (the rule is per OT half), and the two-minute warning is also applied in OT (it exists in the NFL's OT period).
- **Table merging rule:** sparse cells (< 200 plays) are merged with the ordinal neighbor along the table's last key; if a whole prefix group stays under 200 after pooling, that level is dropped and the lookup backs off to the table without the last key, down to the marginal. Implemented in `models/tables.py` as a level hierarchy.
- **D4 split in two** because sacks and scrambles have no air yards in the data: `dropback_outcome` (throw / sack / scramble, keyed by zone x down x distance) then `throw_outcome` (complete / incomplete / interception, keyed by air-yards bin x zone). Together they give the five spec classes.
- **Yardage tables use exact-yard classes estimated with Kaplan-Meier censoring at the goal line** (D3 air yards, D5 YAC, D7 scramble yards, D8 rush yards) instead of spec bins plus within-bin draws. Diagnosis: pooling plays across a field zone and then capping at the goal line truncates twice (a run from the 2 can never show 5 yards in the data), which made the simulated TD rate per play 15-50% too low in every zone (own 0.22% vs 0.40%, red zone 6.9% vs 8.9%, inside the 10 26% vs 31%) and points 2.4 per team-game short. Touchdowns (and end-zone throws, where nflfastR records air yards equal to the yardline) are treated as right-censored observations, so the table holds the uncensored distribution and the engine caps at the yardline. The spec's bins are still defined in `models/buckets.py` for the Phase 2 models.
- **D5 YAC is keyed by air bin x field zone** (spec: air bin only), for the same truncation reason.
- **D16 clock key splits "under 2:00" by half** (H1 vs H2/OT): leading teams hurry at the end of the first half but bleed the clock in Q4; pooling both gave Q4 hurry-ups by the leading team.
- **Timeouts are sampled from empirical tables (D16b), replacing the spec's always-call heuristic.** With the heuristic the trailing team called a timeout after every running-clock play: 3.1 timeouts per game in the last two minutes of Q4 vs 1.6 real, and 7 extra offensive plays per game (plays per team-game 65.7 vs 62.6). `timeout_after` (clock stopper x lead x under-2:00 half) gives the probability a timeout follows the play; `timeout_runoff` (clock stopper x under-2:00 half) gives the snap-to-snap seconds when one does. nflfastR attaches the play's duration to the timeout row, so the two rows' `seconds_elapsed` are summed. Under 2:00 the trailing team is charged the timeout, otherwise the offense. The spec's "take a timeout before a last-second field goal" rule is kept as an explicit override.
- **Out of bounds is sampled** from an empirical table (play type x lead x under-2:00 half) and stops the clock only inside the rule windows (last 2:00 of H1, last 5:00 of H2/OT). Hurry-up offenses go out of bounds about 50% more often than average, which the fixed per-play-type rate missed.
- **D17 pre-snap penalty** is built from every nullified play (false starts, offsides, holding, DPI, ...) and the assessed yards are drawn from the empirical distribution per side (mean 6.5 offense, 7.7 defense) rather than a flat 5; the defensive automatic-first-down rate is empirical (68%). The down is replayed on both sides, half the distance to the goal applies, and from the 1 no yardage can be assessed. Known gap: penalties on plays that stand (0.9 per team-game, about 11 yards each) are not modeled.
- **D13 kickoffs** come from `kickoff_seasons` (2025 only, the 35-yard touchback era) and include a 0.8% "kicking team keeps the ball" outcome (onside kicks and recovered kicks), placed at the kicking team's 45 (`yardline_100 = 55`, assumption). No onside-kick strategy. Return TDs are empirical (0.2%).
- **Free kick after a safety:** only 55 free kicks in the window, under the 200-play rule, so the receiver starts at the settings default (`yardline_100 = 60`).
- **D9 fumbles gained a third class, `yes_td`** (scoop-and-score, 6.5% of lost fumbles). The spec's "no return" dropped about 0.35 points per game of defensive touchdowns. Other lost fumbles give the defense the ball at the spot; a fumble into the end zone is a touchback for the defense; a fumble recovered behind the goal line (a real defensive TD) puts the defense at the 1.
- **Interception in the end zone** (air yards reach the goal line) is a touchback at the 20 (`yardline_100 = 80`); other returns use the empirical yards capped one yard short of the end zone unless `pick_six` was sampled.
- **Missed field goal spot:** "inside the 20" is read as the spot of the kick (snap + 7), i.e. the defense takes over at the spot of the kick or the 20, whichever is further from its goal line. A miss from the 15 therefore gives the ball at the 22.
- **No try after a decisive touchdown:** a TD that ends the game on the last play of regulation, or that ends overtime, gets no PAT (NFL rule). Otherwise the PAT is attempted even at 0:00 (e.g. a tying TD).
- **PAT choice (D14)** keyed by score differential after the TD (clipped to +/-16) x time bucket; nflfastR PAT rows already include the six points. Many cells merge across time buckets under the 200-play rule, so late-game two-point behavior is coarse until Phase 2.
- **4th-down "go" play call** uses the 3rd-down row of D1 (D1 is built on downs 1-3 per spec).
- **Kneel rule:** Q4, offense leading, and the remaining half seconds <= 40 x (downs left minus opponent timeouts) + 2 x min(downs left, opponent timeouts); on 4th down only if the opponent has no timeouts and <= 40 s remain. Added: Q2 with <= 20 s and the ball in the offense's own half. Spikes are not simulated (0.25 per game).
- **Untimed down** after a defensive penalty on the last play of a half: not implemented (known gap, step 1.5.5).
- **End-of-period ordering:** the clock sets `period_expired` and the transition (halftime, game over, OT start, new OT period) is applied at the next step unless a PAT is pending, so a score on the final play always counts and gets its try.
- **Clock after a PAT does not run** (no runoff sampled); the kickoff's runoff is sampled from the clock-stopper pool.
- **Box score conventions:** passing yards are completion yards (sack yards tracked separately, the sportsbook settlement convention); scrambles count as rush attempts and rushing yards; `plays` counts pass, run and kneel snaps (penalties, punts and kicks are separate).
- **Phase 1 engine bug found by the targets:** the first-half clock setter dropped 1,800 seconds (half the plays); fixed in `GameState.set_clock`.
- **Acceptance benchmark for margin std:** reported both against the raw real std (14.19, the spec's literal target, missed by 0.006 with the configured seed) and against the real std net of the closing spread (12.63, the equal-teams benchmark, matched at 12.68). Not tuned; see PROGRESS.md.
- **Residual gaps left for Phase 2** (within tolerance, not tuned): points per team-game +0.7 and yards per rush +0.3 (the censoring correction assumes yardage is independent of the exact yardline within a zone, which slightly over-corrects inside the 10); FG attempts -0.14; two-point share 7% vs 10% (merged PAT cells); OT share 4.7% vs 6.1% (no team strength, no end-of-game strategy beyond the tables).
- **Speed:** the reference Python engine runs 1.0 ms per game on the M5 (5,000 games in 5 s).

## Phase 2

- **The spec's `pass_outcome` model (5 classes) is two models:** `dropback_outcome` (throw / sack / scramble, base features) and `throw_outcome` (complete / incomplete / interception given a throw, base + air yards). Sacks and scrambles have no air yards to condition on, and the engine already samples the dropback result before the throw depth (Phase 1 D4 split). Their product is the five-class distribution.
- **Feature builder design:** one `Situation` dataclass is built either from a `GameState` + extra dict or from a `plays.parquet` row, and one `features(model, situation)` function serves both. The equality test (`tests/test_features.py`) builds 1,000 real plays both ways for all 12 models.
- **Base features exactly as specified**, with one reading: `trailing_late` / `leading_late` ("under 8:00 of Q4") also fire in overtime, since OT is late-game behavior. `half_secs` in OT is the OT clock / 1800, matching nflfastR's `half_seconds_remaining`.
- **Era feature** `(season - 2025) / 5` added to `play_call`, `fourth_down`, `pat_choice`, `air_yards`, `fg_make`, `clock_runoff` and the models that reuse the play-call extras. Reason: 2018-2025 pooled base rates drift (pass rate 61.9% -> 60.1%, fourth-down go rate up sharply), and the first fit was systematically miscalibrated on 2025 (run under-predicted by 3-5 points in mid deciles). At simulation time the season is clipped to the last training season (2026 is treated as 2025, no extrapolation).
- **Per-model extras beyond the spec list** (each tried against the Phase 1 table on 2025 validation before adoption):
  - `play_call`: down x log(ydstogo), short-yardage indicators by down, score x under-2:00, trailing/leading-late x under-2:00, score squared, era. Without these the additive logit lost to the 4-key table (0.5688 vs 0.5652); with them it wins (0.5604). `presnap_penalty` and `throw_outcome` reuse the same set.
  - `air_yards`, `rush_yards`: those interactions plus one indicator per yardline from 1 to 20 (then 21-30, 31-50, 51+). `yac`: the same on the room to the goal line (yardline minus air yards), plus air-yard-bin indicators. Reason: the Phase 1 tables beat the smooth logits because they know exactly which yardage bins the field can still hold; the per-yard indicators give the multinomial the same information.
  - `clock_runoff`: clock-stopper x under-2:00, x trailing-late, x leading-late, second-half indicators, pass x stopper, era, and punt / field-goal / penalty indicators (dead-ball procedures differ by play kind).
  - `fg_make`: distance cubed and era (kickers improved over the window).
  - `pat_choice`: a second-half indicator next to `half_secs` (the spec's two features cannot tell the end of the first half from the end of the game).
- **Goal-line masking for the yardage models:** at inference the engine zeroes classes whose lower edge exceeds the yards available (to the goal line, or to the goal line minus air yards for YAC) and renormalizes. The models are trained on capped data, so their probability for the "reach the goal line" class already contains the touchdowns; letting the engine sample an impossible bin and cap it would double count. The same mask is applied when the report scores the models.
- **Kickoff runoff is its own table** (`kickoff_runoff`, built from `kickoff_seasons`): the 2024-25 kickoff rule change flipped touchbacks (0 s) into returns (5-10 s), which neither the pooled 2018-2025 clock model nor a linear era term can represent. The trained clock model and the Phase 1 clock table now exclude kickoffs; the top decile of the clock model's calibration had been 17 points overconfident because of them.
- **Penalty yards are a separate table** (`penalty_yards`, keyed by side) rather than a within-bin draw attached to the penalty model, so a trained `presnap_penalty` model can be swapped in without losing the yardage draw.
- **Validation protocol:** last training season (2025) is the validation season; the comparison tables are rebuilt from 2021-2024 so neither competitor sees validation plays. Standardization uses training-season means and stds for the validation fit, all-season stats for the final refit. The validation-season fit is saved under `artifacts/models/val/` so the report can be rebuilt without retraining (`nflsim train --evaluate-only`).
- **Calibration measure for multinomial models:** deciles of the predicted probability of the modal class vs. how often that class occurred (the spec's decile rule is defined for a single probability). Binary models use P(class 1).
- **Win-probability filter:** `wp` (nflfastR pre-snap win probability) between 2% and 98%, skipped for `play_call`, `fourth_down`, `pat_choice` and `clock_runoff` as the spec allows.
- **Kept as tables:** D6 sack yards, D7 scramble yards, D10 interception returns, D12 punts, D13 kickoffs, D15 try results, plus the Phase 1 additions (timeouts, out of bounds, fumble-return TDs, penalty yards, kickoff runoff).
- **Bug caught by the swapped-in acceptance run:** the first `fumble_lost` training set had no designed runs (a `~is_in` filter on the nullable `pass_outcome` column dropped every run row), so runs fumbled at the pooled 1.1% rate instead of 0.65% and lost fumbles per team-game rose to 0.585 vs 0.507 real. Filter made null-safe, model retrained on 200k rows (val log loss 0.0431 vs table 0.0435).
- **Home advantage now appears from `is_home`** in the trained models (simulated home margin +1.35 with AVG vs AVG, real 2021-25 +2.06). Phase 3 step 3.3 measures and, if needed, adjusts it; nothing was tuned here.

## Phase 3

- **Rating definitions follow the spec table**, with these readings: `pace` is the per-game median `seconds_elapsed` on offensive snaps in neutral situations, then exponentially averaged across games weighted by those snaps (a median is not additive across games); `proe`, `cpoe` and `fourth_aggr` use the Phase 2 situation-only models saved under `artifacts/models/situation/` as the "expected" reference. Those reference models are league-wide and contain no team identity, so they do not leak team information, but they were fit on 2018-2025 (Phase 7's walk-forward retrains them per test season).
- **Weighting:** `rating = (prior x prior_den + sum lam^k num_g) / (prior_den + sum lam^k den_g)`, with `lam = 0.5^(1/8)`, k = games since that game (newest = 0), and num/den the per-play sums so each game is weighted by its plays. The prior is last season's final rating regressed one third toward the league mean of final ratings, and `prior_den` is six games' worth of the league-average denominator for that stat. The prior is not decayed; its share shrinks as games accumulate. 2018 has no prior (no 2017 data), so 2018 week 1 is all zeros (league average).
- **As-of date:** a team's rating for (season, week) uses its games strictly before its own game date in that week, or before the week's first kickoff if it is on a bye. Z-scores are taken across all 32 teams within the (season, week). Playoff weeks (nflverse `game_type` WC/DIV/CON/SB, weeks 19-22) are included; the first build filtered on `POST` and silently left every playoff training row at zero ratings, caught by the state-vs-row feature test.
- **Leakage tests:** (1) 200 random (team, week) rows rebuilt from per-game stats restricted to `game_date < as-of date` match exactly (one row is also rebuilt from the raw plays end to end); (2) adding 50 EPA to one week-9 play of 2025 changes no rating at weeks <= 9 and does change later weeks.
- **Team features per model** follow the spec table; the spec's `pass_outcome` features are split between `dropback_outcome` (sack_rate, pass_epa; sack_rate_forced, pass_epa_allowed) and `throw_outcome` (cpoe, pass_epa; int_rate_forced, pass_epa_allowed) because of the Phase 2 split. Weather (`roof_dome`, `wind_c`) added to `throw_outcome` and `air_yards` per step 3.4; the manual weather file is `data/inputs/weather_<season>_wk<week>.csv` (missing file = neutral weather, with a warning).
- **Training join** is on (posteam, season, week) and (defteam, season, week) from the as-of table only; rows with no row in the as-of table (2018 week 1) get zeros, never end-of-season values.
- **Backtest runner** (`nflsim phase3-check`): 2025 regular-season games simulated week by week with that week's as-of ratings, 2,000 sims each, one process per core, per-game seed = settings seed + CRC32(game_id).
- **Spread sign convention verified:** nflverse `spread_line` is the home team's expected margin (positive = home favored); its correlation with `result` (home minus away) over 2021-25 is +0.46.
- **Home-field offset (step 3.3):** with only `is_home` in the models, AVG vs AVG simulated a home margin of +0.95 (5,000 games; real 2021-25 regular season, non-neutral: +2.06 on average, per-season range 1.57 to 2.71). Added `home_logit_offset` in `config/settings.yaml` (one value per model): the home offense gets +h/2 and the away offense -h/2 on the logit of "complete" in `throw_outcome` and along a longer-gain direction in `rush_yards` ((yards - 4)/10, clipped to +/-1); neutral sites get nothing. Fitted by simulation at h in {0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3} (3,000 games each): margin = 0.90 + 19.1 h, so h = 0.06 for +2.06. Set to 0.06 for both models. Points, completion rate and yards per rush do not move with h (the offset is symmetric), only the home/away split.
- **Speed note:** the trained team models cost 19 ms per game in pure Python (was 8 ms with the Phase 2 models and 1 ms with tables), so a 2,000-sim season backtest runs multiprocess (about 10 minutes on 10 cores). Phase 5's Numba engine remains the plan for weekly 10,000-sim runs.
- **Standardization changed from per-week z-scores to a per-season scale:** ratings are centered on the league mean for that week but divided by the spread (std across teams) of the *previous* season's final raw ratings (first season: within-week std). Reason: a per-week std re-inflates week-1 ratings (regressed priors with no games) to the same size as sharp late-season ratings, which blurs the models' per-unit effect. With the new scale, week-1 spread is 0.68 and reaches about 1.0 by week 4. Still league-relative and leak-free (last season's finals are known before the season). The spec's purpose for the z-score (comparable scales across seasons) is preserved.
- **Smoothing parameters kept at the spec defaults** (half-life 8, prior worth 6 games, regression 0.33) after a grid over half-life {4..24} x prior games {6..36} x regression {0.33, 0.5} scored by out-of-sample 2025 R^2 of a game-level regression of margin on the rating differences: the defaults are within 0.002 of the best R^2 (0.155) and every setting predicts with a std near 6.2 and a slope near 0.9. `prior_games` is now a config key.
- **Team-quality spread diagnosis (Phase 3 acceptance, std of simulated margins vs closing spreads):** the simulated spread is 4.0 vs 6.2. Checks done, in order: (1) regularization is not the cause (team coefficients are the same at C = 0.01 and C = 10); (2) the per-play models are calibrated within rating bins for yards per carry, completion rate, touchdown rate inside the 20, third-down conversion and first-down rate (actual vs model agree to within noise in every bin); (3) a game-level regression on the same 13 ratings predicts margins out of sample with std 6.2 and slope 0.9, and the part running through scrimmage-play EPA alone is worth about 6 points of spread, so the information is in the ratings. See PROGRESS.md for the per-rating sensitivity comparison and the conclusion.
- **Per-rating sensitivity (8,000 neutral-site games per rating, +1 unit on the home side):** points per unit in the sim vs the game-level regression partials: pass_epa +0.80 vs +1.70, rush_epa +0.71 vs +1.68, cpoe +0.92 vs +0.57, explosive +0.44 vs +0.38, pass_epa_allowed -0.61 vs -0.95, rush_epa_allowed -0.39 vs -1.00. A linear combination of the sim's per-unit effects reproduces the backtest's simulated margins (correlation 0.98, std 4.4 vs 4.0), so the compression is entirely in how much margin each rating unit buys. Adding the broad EPA ratings to the other models (air yards, rush yards, play call, 4th down, dropback, throw, YAC) improves validation log loss by at most 0.0008, i.e. the play-level models already extract the ratings' play-level signal; the game-level relationship is steeper than the play-by-play sum.
- **Calibration layer `team_strength.input_scale` (simulation path only):** regressing actual margins on the sim-implied margin gives a slope of 1.43 (se 0.085) on 2019-2024 and 1.39 on 2025 held out, so the rating inputs are multiplied by 1.43 when a `GameState` is turned into features (never when training rows are). This is a fitted calibration (like temperature scaling) tested out of sample, not a value chosen to hit the acceptance band; it is flagged for Luke because it is the one place where the engine's team effect is not purely learned play by play. Phase 7's walk-forward backtest re-estimates it per test season.

## Phase 4

- **No FanDuel client exists** (noted at Setup), so step 4.1.3 (props posted = active) is skipped until Phase 6 adds the client; the hook is the `overrides` file, which always wins.
- **Actives:** weekly roster rows with status `ACT` for QB/RB/WR/TE/K, minus `Out` and `Doubtful` on the injury report (`Questionable` kept and warned about), then overrides. nflverse roster statuses RES/DEV/INA/CUT/RET etc. are treated as not active. The starting QB is the override, else the active QB with the most exponentially weighted recent dropbacks (the as-of table's `n_qb_cpoe` count), which approximates "most dropbacks in the last 3 games".
- **Participation for usage** comes from snap counts (PFR ids mapped to GSIS ids through the players table, 91% coverage) so a player who played without a touch still gets weight in his share history; players with touches but no snap match are included from the play-by-play itself.
- **Shares** are ratios of exponentially weighted sums (half-life 4 games, `player_usage.half_life_games`) over the player's games before the as-of date, including last season's games through the decay (two seasons at most). Air-yard bands: short < 5, intermediate 5-14, deep >= 15. `bin_fit` = (band targets + 20 x overall share) / (band team targets + 20) divided by the overall share, floored at 0.05.
- **Renormalization:** the share missing among the actives is redistributed in proportion to 50/50 own share and the position-slot prior; if actives' shares exceed 1 they are scaled down. Slots are ranks by snap share within position among the actives; the priors are league averages 2021-2025 by (position, slot) (WR1 22%, WR2 18%, WR3 13%, TE1 15%, RB1 31% of carries, ...). Players with fewer than 20 team plays of history get the slot prior outright. Backup QBs get no targets; their designed-run carries stay.
- **Efficiency** ("over expected") uses the Phase 2 situation-only models as the expectation (same reference as Phase 3's PROE/CPOE), exponentially weighted with the team half-life (8 games, the spec does not give one for efficiency) and shrunk toward 0 with k = 50 pseudo-plays (kickers k = 100). Expected rush yards / YAC use the masked class means of the situation models.
- **Player features per model:** dropback_outcome (qb_sack_rate, qb_scramble_rate); throw_outcome (rec_catch_rate_oe, qb_cpoe, qb_int_rate); yac (rec_yac_oe); rush_yards (rush_yards_oe); air_yards (qb_adot); fg_make (kicker_fg_oe, step 4.5 implemented). Training rows join the as-of values by the play's receiver / rusher / passer / kicker id; missing = 0.
- **Player choice order** follows step 4.3: the QB's effects feed the dropback and depth models, the target is chosen after the depth is sampled (share x band fit, red-zone blend 50/50 inside the 20), then the outcome and YAC use the receiver's effects; the carrier is chosen before rush yards.
- **QB blend rule (step 4.4):** when the starting QB is not the QB with the most weighted recent dropbacks, the team's pass_epa, cpoe, sack_rate and adot ratings are blended 50/50 with the new QB's own shrunk over-expected rates mapped to the team z scale (cpoe 2.5 points per z, sack rate 1.5 points per z, pass_epa 0.6 z per z of cpoe) when he has >= 100 weighted dropbacks, otherwise with a backup prior (pass_epa -0.8, cpoe -0.6, sack_rate +0.4 z). The blend is printed in the run output. The mapping constants are judgment calls recorded here; Phase 7 can re-estimate them.
- **Box score:** passing yards = completion yards credited to the passer and the receiver (sack yards stay in the team's `sack_yards`), scrambles are QB rushes, fumbles lost go to the ball carrier, anytime TD = any rushing or receiving TD.
- **Calibration sample:** the spec asks for players with at least 20 appearances, but weeks 4-18 of one season allow at most 15, so the check uses at least 8 appearances (`--min-games`). Actuals come from the play-by-play (passing yards per passer, receiving yards and receptions per receiver, rushing yards per rusher including scrambles).
- **Bug caught by the model report:** the first `dropback_outcome` retrain with QB effects showed a validation log loss of 0.268 (from 0.448), far too good. Cause: nflfastR records a scrambling QB in `rusher_player_id` with no `passer_player_id`, so scramble rows joined no QB effect (0) while every other dropback joined the QB's negative shrunk scramble rate, and the model learned the gap (target leakage through the join). The QB id for dropbacks is now `passer_player_id`, falling back to `rusher_player_id` on scrambles, both when building the QB's history and when joining training rows; the six player models were retrained.

## Phase 5

- **Fast engine structure:** every decision is a "slot" packed either as a dense cumulative-probability table over the full key domain (the Phase 1 back-off is baked in at pack time; verified equal to the hierarchical lookup on random keys) or as a trained model (means, stds, coefficient matrix, intercepts, yardage mask edges, home-offset direction). The jitted feature builder is a line-by-line mirror of `models/features.py`; `tests/test_fast_engine.py` asserts exact equality of features and probabilities on random states for every trained model, so the two builders cannot drift silently.
- **RNG:** a splitmix64 generator with one 64-bit state per simulation, seeded from the game seed and the simulation index, so results do not depend on the thread count or scheduling. The reference engine uses NumPy's default generator; the engines are equivalent in distribution, not stream for stream.
- **Per-game seed** is `settings.seed + CRC32(game_id)` (the spec's `seed + hash(game_id)`; Python's `hash` of a string is salted per process, so CRC32 is used to make it reproducible).
- **Player arrays:** players get integer indices per side (0 = home, 1 = away) in the order of the team's player dict; the player stat array is `[n_sims, 2 * n_max, 16]` with the home side first, mapped back to ids through `player_ids` in the saved `.npz`. Efficiency effects are stored per player in a fixed 9-slot order and assembled per play exactly as the reference engine does (QB effects for dropbacks and depth, receiver effects after the target is chosen, rusher effect for runs, kicker effect for field goals).
- **Half scores are not tracked** in either engine, so first-half markets are skipped (spec allows).
- **Team totals** are produced for lines 10.5-44.5 in half-point steps (the spec lists team totals but gives no range).
- **Player props** store the 5th-95th percentiles and `P(over)` at every half-point line between them for passing yards / TDs / interceptions, rushing yards / attempts, receiving yards / receptions and longest reception / rush, plus anytime TD.
