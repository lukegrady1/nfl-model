## Phase 0

- **Extra columns kept** beyond the spec list: `drive`, `fixed_drive` (spec asks for them), `game_date`, `home_score`, `away_score`, `wp`, `vegas_wp` (Phase 2 filters on pre-snap win probability), `desc`, `td_team`, `defensive_two_point_conv`, `return_team`, kick/punt detail flags. Reason: validation and the Phase 1 tables need them; nothing was removed from the spec list.
- **Marker rows** (`play_type` null: GAME, END QUARTER, END GAME, Two-Minute Warning) are used to compute `seconds_elapsed` and `next_is_two_minute_warning`, then dropped from `plays.parquet`.
- **`seconds_elapsed`** = this row's `game_seconds_remaining` minus the next row's (any row type, including timeout rows, so a play followed by a timeout records only the play's own duration). Null across halftime, the end of regulation and OT period boundaries. Clipped to [0, 60].
- **Clock-stopper approximation:** out of bounds counts as a stop only when the clock at the snap is under 2:00 of the first half or 5:00 of the second half (NFL rule: otherwise the clock restarts on the ready-for-play signal). Also treated as stops: incomplete passes, spikes, scores, changes of possession (interception, lost fumble, punt, kickoff, failed 4th down), accepted penalties, and plays followed by the two-minute warning.
- **nflfastR codes the receiving team as `posteam` on kickoff rows.** Start-spot tables use `next_scrim_posteam` / `next_scrim_yardline` (the next scrimmage snap), so kickoffs and punts are handled identically.
- **Two-point tries** (`two_point_attempt == 1`, play_type pass/run) are classified `two_point` before the pass/run rules so they never enter the play-call or outcome tables.
- **Score check mismatches** (3 games): all are 2-point swaps where a safety was scored by the original offense after a same-play turnover. The validation attributes safeties to `defteam`; the real play has two possession changes. Left as logged mismatches (0.13% < 1%).
- **Summary definitions:** offensive plays per team-game = pass + run + kneel + spike; pass call rate = pass / (pass + run) excluding kneels and spikes; sack and scramble rates per dropback; interception and completion rates per attempt (complete + incomplete + interception). Extra target columns were added (`margin_std`, `ot_share`, `tie_share`, `home_margin_mean`, `fg_make_rate`, `drives_per_team_game`, `two_point_share`) because the Phase 1 acceptance needs OT share and margin std.
- **2026 is in progress** (79 regular-season games through week 6 in the cache). It is always refreshed on ingest and is included in `plays.parquet`, but the Phase 1 tables use 2021-2025 only (`table_seasons`).

## Phase 1

- **Overtime rules check:** the official rulebook pages on operations.nfl.com returned 404 to the fetcher, so the config values were verified against several secondary sources that quote Rule 16 (ESPN, NBC/PFT, Fox Sports explainers for 2025-26). Confirmed: regular season = one 10-minute period, both teams get a possession unless the first possession ends with a defensive touchdown or a safety, sudden death after both have possessed, ties allowed, 2 timeouts per team; playoffs = 15-minute periods until there is a winner, 3 timeouts. Settings kept as written. Simplifications: playoff OT timeouts reset every period (the rule is per OT half), and the two-minute warning is also applied in OT (it exists in the NFL's OT period).
- **Table merging rule:** sparse cells (< 200 plays) are merged with the ordinal neighbor along the table's last key; if a whole prefix group stays under 200 after pooling, that level is dropped and the lookup backs off to the table without the last key, down to the marginal. Implemented in `models/tables.py` as a level hierarchy.
- **D4 split in two** because sacks and scrambles have no air yards in the data: `dropback_outcome` (throw / sack / scramble, keyed by zone x down x distance) then `throw_outcome` (complete / incomplete / interception, keyed by air-yards bin x zone). Together they give the five spec classes.
- **Yardage tables use exact-yard classes estimated with Kaplan-Meier censoring at the goal line** (D3 air yards, D5 YAC, D7 scramble yards, D8 rush yards) instead of spec bins plus within-bin draws. Diagnosis: pooling plays across a field zone and then capping at the goal line truncates twice (a run from the 2 can never show 5 yards in the data), which made the simulated TD rate per play 15-50% too low in every zone (own 0.22% vs 0.40%, red zone 6.9% vs 8.9%, inside the 10 26% vs 31%) and points 2.4 per team-game short. Touchdowns (and end-zone throws, where nflfastR records air yards equal to the yardline) are treated as right-censored observations, so the table holds the uncensored distribution and the engine caps at the yardline. The spec's bins are still defined in `models/buckets.py` for the Phase 2 models.
- **D5 YAC is keyed by air bin x field zone** (spec: air bin only), for the same truncation reason.
- **D16 clock key splits "under 2:00" by half** (H1 vs H2/OT): leading teams hurry at the end of the first half but bleed the clock in Q4; pooling both gave Q4 hurry-ups by the leading team.
- **Timeouts are sampled from empirical tables (D16b), replacing the spec's always-call heuristic.** With the heuristic the trailing team called a timeout after every running-clock play: 3.1 timeouts per game in the last two minutes of Q4 vs 1.6 real, and 7 extra offensive plays per game (plays per team-game 65.7 vs 62.6). `timeout_after` (clock stopper x lead x under-2:00 half) gives the probability a timeout follows the play; `timeout_runoff` (clock stopper x under-2:00 half) gives the snap-to-snap seconds when one does. nflfastR attaches the play's duration to the timeout row, so the two rows' `seconds_elapsed` are summed. Under 2:00 the trailing team is charged the timeout, otherwise the offense. The spec's "take a timeout before a last-second field goal" rule is kept as an explicit override.
- **Out of bounds is sampled** from an empirical table (play type x lead x under-2:00 half) and stops the clock only inside the rule windows (last 2:00 of H1, last 5:00 of H2/OT). Hurry-up offenses go out of bounds about 50% more often than average, which the fixed per-play-type rate missed.
- **D17 pre-snap penalty** is built from every nullified play (false starts, offsides, holding, DPI, ...) and the assessed yards are drawn from the empirical distribution per side (mean 6.5 offense, 7.7 defense) rather than a flat 5; the defensive automatic-first-down rate is empirical (68%). The down is replayed on both sides, half the distance to the goal applies, and from the 1 no yardage can be assessed. Known gap: penalties on plays that stand (0.9 per team-game, about 11 yards each) are not modeled.
- **D13 kickoffs** come from `kickoff_seasons` (2025 only, the 35-yard touchback era) and include a 0.8% "kicking team keeps the ball" outcome (onside kicks and recovered kicks), placed at the kicking team's 45 (`yardline_100 = 55`, assumption). No onside-kick strategy. Return TDs are empirical (0.2%).
- **Free kick after a safety:** only 55 free kicks in the window, under the 200-play rule, so the receiver starts at the settings default (`yardline_100 = 60`).
- **D9 fumbles gained a third class, `yes_td`** (scoop-and-score, 6.5% of lost fumbles). The spec's "no return" dropped about 0.35 points per game of defensive touchdowns. Other lost fumbles give the defense the ball at the spot; a fumble into the end zone is a touchback for the defense; a fumble recovered behind the goal line (a real defensive TD) puts the defense at the 1.
- **Interception in the end zone** (air yards reach the goal line) is a touchback at the 20 (`yardline_100 = 80`); other returns use the empirical yards capped one yard short of the end zone unless `pick_six` was sampled.
- **Missed field goal spot:** "inside the 20" is read as the spot of the kick (snap + 7), i.e. the defense takes over at the spot of the kick or the 20, whichever is further from its goal line. A miss from the 15 therefore gives the ball at the 22.
- **No try after a decisive touchdown:** a TD that ends the game on the last play of regulation, or that ends overtime, gets no PAT (NFL rule). Otherwise the PAT is attempted even at 0:00 (e.g. a tying TD).
- **PAT choice (D14)** keyed by score differential after the TD (clipped to +/-16) x time bucket; nflfastR PAT rows already include the six points. Many cells merge across time buckets under the 200-play rule, so late-game two-point behavior is coarse until Phase 2.
- **4th-down "go" play call** uses the 3rd-down row of D1 (D1 is built on downs 1-3 per spec).
- **Kneel rule:** Q4, offense leading, and the remaining half seconds <= 40 x (downs left minus opponent timeouts) + 2 x min(downs left, opponent timeouts); on 4th down only if the opponent has no timeouts and <= 40 s remain. Added: Q2 with <= 20 s and the ball in the offense's own half. Spikes are not simulated (0.25 per game).
- **Untimed down** after a defensive penalty on the last play of a half: not implemented (known gap, step 1.5.5).
- **End-of-period ordering:** the clock sets `period_expired` and the transition (halftime, game over, OT start, new OT period) is applied at the next step unless a PAT is pending, so a score on the final play always counts and gets its try.
- **Clock after a PAT does not run** (no runoff sampled); the kickoff's runoff is sampled from the clock-stopper pool.
- **Box score conventions:** passing yards are completion yards (sack yards tracked separately, the sportsbook settlement convention); scrambles count as rush attempts and rushing yards; `plays` counts pass, run and kneel snaps (penalties, punts and kicks are separate).
- **Phase 1 engine bug found by the targets:** the first-half clock setter dropped 1,800 seconds (half the plays); fixed in `GameState.set_clock`.
- **Acceptance benchmark for margin std:** reported both against the raw real std (14.19, the spec's literal target, missed by 0.006 with the configured seed) and against the real std net of the closing spread (12.63, the equal-teams benchmark, matched at 12.68). Not tuned; see PROGRESS.md.
- **Residual gaps left for Phase 2** (within tolerance, not tuned): points per team-game +0.7 and yards per rush +0.3 (the censoring correction assumes yardage is independent of the exact yardline within a zone, which slightly over-corrects inside the 10); FG attempts -0.14; two-point share 7% vs 10% (merged PAT cells); OT share 4.7% vs 6.1% (no team strength, no end-of-game strategy beyond the tables).
- **Speed:** the reference Python engine runs 1.0 ms per game on the M5 (5,000 games in 5 s).
